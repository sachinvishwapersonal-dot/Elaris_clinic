When to see a specialist

Trying for a while without success?

A fertility check-up is usually advised after 12 months of trying if you are under 35, or after 6 months if you are 35 or older.

See a specialist sooner if periods are irregular, you have PCOS or endometriosis, you've had pelvic surgery, or there's a known male factor.

Fertility & IVF

Fertility treatments

Every plan starts with testing both partners. The results decide the treatment, starting with the simplest option likely to help.

Start here

Fertility evaluation

Tests for the woman typically include an ultrasound, AMH and hormone tests, and a tube check (HSG). Men have a semen analysis.

Treatment

Ovulation induction

Tablets or injections to help regular ovulation, with scans to time intercourse. Often the first step for PCOS.

Treatment

IUI (intrauterine insemination)

Washed, concentrated sperm placed in the uterus around ovulation. A short, simple procedure in the clinic.

Treatment

IVF (in vitro fertilisation)

Eggs are collected after hormone stimulation, fertilised in the lab, and an embryo is transferred to the uterus. See each step.

Treatment

ICSI

A single sperm is injected into each egg. Used when sperm count or movement is low, or after fertilisation problems in earlier IVF.

Treatment

Frozen embryo transfer (FET)

Embryos frozen in an earlier cycle are thawed and transferred, without repeating stimulation.

Preservation

Egg & embryo freezing

For women who want to delay pregnancy, or before treatments such as chemotherapy that can affect fertility.

Surgery

Fertility-enhancing surgery

Laparoscopy and hysteroscopy to treat endometriosis, fibroids, polyps or blocked tubes.

Conditions

Common causes we evaluate and treat

PCOS

Irregular or absent ovulation, often treated first with lifestyle changes and ovulation induction.

Endometriosis

Painful periods and reduced fertility, managed with medicines, surgery or IVF.

Low ovarian reserve

A low AMH or egg count. Timely treatment matters most here.

Blocked tubes

Found on HSG or laparoscopy. IVF bypasses the tubes.

Male factor

Low count, movement or shape of sperm, often treated with IUI or ICSI.

Unexplained infertility

Normal test results but no pregnancy. Usually a stepwise plan from IUI to IVF.

Your IVF journey

How an IVF cycle works, step by step

From the start of injections to the pregnancy test usually takes about four to six weeks. Your own plan may differ, and Dr. Vaidya will explain each stage before it begins.

Day ~13Trigger
Day ~15Egg retrieval
Days 15–20Embryo culture
~14 days laterPregnancy test
Book a consultationSee costs
  1. 1–2 visits

    Consultation and tests

    History, ultrasound, AMH and hormone tests, and semen analysis decide whether IVF is right and which protocol to use.

  2. About 10–12 days

    Ovarian stimulation

    Daily hormone injections help several eggs mature. Three to five monitoring scans track how the follicles grow.

  3. 36 hours before retrieval

    Trigger injection

    A final injection prepares the eggs for collection. Timing is exact, so the clinic sets the hour.

  4. 15–20 minutes, same-day discharge

    Egg retrieval

    Eggs are collected through a fine needle under ultrasound guidance, with sedation so you are comfortable.

  5. 3–5 days in the lab

    Fertilisation and embryo culture

    Eggs are fertilised by conventional IVF or ICSI. Embryos grow in the lab, and extra good-quality embryos can be frozen.

  6. A few minutes, no anaesthesia

    Embryo transfer

    A thin catheter places the embryo in the uterus. Most women go back to light routine the same day.

  7. About 14 days after transfer

    Pregnancy test

    A beta-hCG blood test confirms the result. If positive, the first scan follows two to three weeks later.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

When should we see a fertility specialist?

After 12 months of trying if the woman is under 35, or after 6 months if she is 35 or older. Come sooner with irregular periods, PCOS, endometriosis, past pelvic surgery or a known sperm problem.

Do both partners need to come to the first visit?

It helps. Male factors contribute in many cases, and a semen analysis is part of the basic evaluation. If one partner can't come, the first visit can still go ahead.

What is the difference between IUI and IVF?

In IUI, sperm is placed in the uterus and fertilisation happens naturally inside the body. In IVF, eggs are collected and fertilised in the lab, and an embryo is placed in the uterus.

Is IVF painful?

The daily injections are small and most women manage them easily. Egg retrieval is done under sedation. Embryo transfer feels similar to a routine internal examination.

How many clinic visits does an IVF cycle need?

Usually three to five monitoring scans during stimulation, then egg retrieval, embryo transfer and a blood test. Some consultations can be done by video.

Will my visit be kept private?

Yes. Appointment messages only say you have an appointment at Elaris, never the reason. Your reports are seen only by your care team.

Can I consult by video?

Yes, for a first fertility consultation, a gynaecology consultation or a review of reports. Scans and procedures need a clinic visit.

Do you tell the sex of the baby during a scan?

No. Sex determination of the foetus is prohibited under the PC-PNDT Act, 1994, and is not done at Elaris.

How much does IVF cost?

It depends on medicine doses, ICSI and freezing. See the costs page for starting prices. You receive a written estimate before treatment begins.
